# Candidates — The Most Boring Object, Reviewed in One Line

Joke craft: specific, true detail + a twist in the last words. Each candidate is
scored 1–5 against the house judge's five criteria (most important first):

1. On-topic: one-line review of a boring everyday object, funny and true.
2. Laugh comes from a real, recognisable detail of the object (not a generic gag).
3. True: the observation matches how the object actually behaves.
4. One line, ≤ 200 characters, every word earning its place.
5. Lands fast: the turn comes early, the last words sharpen it.

## Candidates

### C1 — Rubber doorstop (137 chars)
> Our rubber doorstop has spent three years wedged under the same door, unnoticed, unpaid, and utterly unbothered — the most successful employee this house has ever had.
- 1: 5 | 2: 4 | 3: 4 | 4: 4 | 5: 4 — Total 21
- Notes: "unnoticed, unpaid, unbothered" is true and specific; the employee twist lands, but "three years" is invented precision and the twist is a little long.

### C2 — Paperclip (151 chars)
> This paperclip has held the same three pages together since 2019, never once asking for a promotion, a raise, or even a thank-you — middle management's spirit animal.
- 1: 4 | 2: 3 | 3: 4 | 4: 3 | 5: 4 — Total 18
- Notes: the holding-pages detail is true, but "promotion / raise / spirit animal" is a generic office gag that would fit a stapler too.

### C3 — Tea towel (127 chars)
> The tea towel hangs by the sink, damp since Tuesday, drying nothing, judging no one, achieving the exact nothing it was bought for.
- 1: 5 | 2: 5 | 3: 5 | 4: 5 | 5: 3 — Total 23
- Notes: "damp since Tuesday, drying nothing" is painfully true and specific. Weakness: the ending restates the premise instead of twisting it — no real turn.

### C4 — Rubber band (139 chars)
> This rubber band has been stretched around the same drawer of spare batteries for so long it has forgotten how to snap — it now just sighs and holds on.
- 1: 4 | 2: 4 | 3: 3 | 4: 4 | 5: 3 — Total 18
- Notes: old rubber bands do go slack, but "sighs" anthropomorphises past the truth, and the ending trails off instead of sharpening.

### C5 — Wire coat hanger (134 chars)
> The wire coat hanger in my closet holds one shirt, bends a little more each year, and has never once complained — unlike the shirt, which is also boring.
- 1: 4 | 2: 4 | 3: 4 | 4: 3 | 5: 2 — Total 17
- Notes: the bending detail is real, but the ending ("unlike the shirt") is a second joke that lands nowhere.

### C6 — Kitchen sponge (145 chars)
> Our kitchen sponge absorbs everything, smells of last week, and gets replaced only when it starts to smell like this sentence — which is to say, eventually.
- 1: 4 | 2: 4 | 3: 4 | 4: 3 | 5: 3 — Total 18
- Notes: the smell detail is true, but the meta-joke ("smell like this sentence") is cleverness about the review, not about the sponge.

### C7 — Doormat (142 chars) ★ WINNER
> The doormat says WELCOME in faded letters, has been stepped on ten thousand times, and has never once been thanked — a doormat in every sense.
- 1: 5 | 2: 5 | 3: 5 | 4: 5 | 5: 5 — Total 25
- Notes: every detail is checkable and true (faded WELCOME, stepped on constantly, never thanked); the object's own name becomes the punchline, so the dullness itself is the joke; the twist is the last four words.

### C8 — Charging cable (143 chars)
> The white charging cable by the bed is exactly long enough to reach the pillow and exactly short enough to yank the phone off it at 2 a.m. — every single night.
- 1: 4 | 2: 4 | 3: 4 | 4: 4 | 5: 3 — Total 19
- Notes: true and recognisable, but a charging cable is a frustration object, not a boring one — it fails criterion 1 against a true doormat.

## Iteration

**Round 1 (pick):** C7 wins on total (25) and on the first-differentiating criterion — its laugh comes from the object's own name and its actual life (stepped on, ignored), where C3 (23) has the truth but no turn.

**Round 2 (tighten):** Draft v1 read "Our doormat … it is a doormat in every sense of the word." Cut "Our" → "The" and cut "it is … of the word" → "a doormat in every sense." Same punchline, 12 fewer characters, no wasted words.

**Round 3 (final check):** Read aloud: the turn lands in the last four words; nothing after it trails off. 142 characters (python-verified), one line, no trailing newline in the program output. No recycled numbers from earlier bounties; no text copied from any previous entry.

## Final entry (142 characters)

> The doormat says WELCOME in faded letters, has been stepped on ten thousand times, and has never once been thanked — a doormat in every sense.
